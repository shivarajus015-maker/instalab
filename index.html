LAB 03 · Matter / ಪದಾರ್ಥ

⚛️ Particle Energy Model

Matter is made of particles. In many substances, higher temperature means greater average particle motion. This is a visual analogy, not a precise molecular simulation.

Think: how could heating change a solid, liquid or gas? Details differ by material and conditions.

LAB 04 · Motion / ಚಲನೆ

🚀 Rocket Height Calculator

Explore a simplified vertical-launch model. If air resistance and continuing thrust are ignored, maximum height can be estimated from speed and gravity.

🚀
45.9 m
Formula: h = v² / (2g), where g ≈ 9.81 m/s².

Real rockets are more complex: thrust changes, fuel is used, air resistance matters, and orbit is not the same as a straight vertical launch.

Tools and everyday science

Science Materials Gallery

Learn what each item does, where it is used, how to explore it and which safety rules matter.

🌡️

Thermometer

Purpose: Measures temperature in Celsius or another scale.

Where used: Weather stations, cooking, science labs and suitable health settings.

Try it: Compare safe cool and warm samples using teacher-approved equipment.

Safety: Do not touch hot samples or use broken glass thermometers.

🧲

Magnet

Purpose: Creates a magnetic field and attracts some materials, especially iron-containing objects.

Where used: Compasses, speakers, motors, magnetic catches and recycling systems.

Try it: Predict which safe classroom objects will be attracted, then test and record.

Safety: Keep strong magnets away from small children and sensitive electronics. Never swallow a magnet.

🔋

Battery, wire and bulb

Purpose: Shows that current needs a complete circuit path.

Where used: Torches, toys, sensors and portable devices.

Try it: Draw a battery-switch-bulb circuit and compare an open and closed switch.

Safety: Use only low-voltage learning kits. Never experiment with wall sockets or mains electricity.

🧪

pH indicator paper

Purpose: Gives an approximate indication of acidity or alkalinity.

Where used: School science, water testing and quality checks.

Try it: Use teacher-approved samples and compare the colour with the kit chart.

Safety: Never taste or mix unknown liquids; follow instructions.

🔍

Magnifying glass

Purpose: A convex lens can make nearby objects appear larger.

Where used: Field observation, reading small print and examining textures.

Try it: Compare a leaf or fabric with and without magnification.

Safety: Never focus sunlight through a lens or look directly at the Sun.

⚖️

Balance and measuring cylinder

Purpose: A balance measures mass; a graduated cylinder measures liquid volume.

Where used: Science labs, kitchens, manufacturing and research.

Try it: Estimate first, measure second and compare the results.

Safety: Handle glassware carefully; never use lab containers for food or drink.

Wonder about the world

Amazing Science Facts

Understand why a fact happens, not just what it says. Open each explanation for more detail.

⚡

Lightning heats air intensely

Air in a lightning channel can briefly reach around 30,000°C—hotter than the Sun’s visible surface.

Lightning rapidly heats air. The air expands suddenly and creates a pressure wave we hear as thunder. Lightning is dangerous; go indoors during a storm.

🌌

Sound needs a medium

Sound cannot travel through a perfect vacuum because there are no particles to pass on vibrations.

They use radio communication. Radio waves are electromagnetic waves and can travel through vacuum; sound waves need matter such as air, water or solids.

🐙

Some ocean animals make light

Bioluminescence is light produced by a living organism through a chemical reaction.

Depending on the species, light can attract prey, confuse predators or support communication. Different organisms use it in different ways.

🌈

White light contains many colours

A prism can separate white light into a spectrum because different wavelengths refract by different amounts.

Use a prism with a teacher-approved light source and observe colours on a screen. Never look directly at the Sun through a prism or optical device.

🌿

Plants store light energy

Photosynthesis uses light energy to help convert carbon dioxide and water into sugars, releasing oxygen.

Plants also need water, mineral nutrients and suitable temperatures. Light alone does not guarantee healthy growth.

🧊

Ice floats on liquid water

At ordinary pressure, ice is less dense than liquid water because its crystal structure holds molecules farther apart.

Floating ice can insulate the water below, helping aquatic life survive cold conditions. This unusual density behaviour matters in lakes and ecosystems.

Karnataka Textbook Explorer

For classes 6–10, use the official Karnataka State textbook portal. Choose class and medium there, open the relevant Science chapter, then use these simulations to reinforce concepts.

📚 How to use the textbook portal

  1. Open the official Karnataka textbook website.
  2. Select your current class: 6, 7, 8, 9 or 10.
  3. Choose Kannada or English medium, then Science and the chapter.
  4. Write key points, try a related virtual lab and complete the quiz.
